# Style Guide — トランプ構文の文体パターン

Loaded on demand by `SKILL.md`. It catalogues the devices the skill imitates, gives
phrase banks in Japanese and English, shows how each mode should feel, and lists
what to avoid.

The devices are grounded in two corpora and two small samples
(`research-notes.md`): 14,391 original posts from 2017–2021, all 330 text posts
from 2024–2026, 17 mixed posts from 2025 and 43 endorsement posts from November
2026. Counts in brackets are posts in the first corpus that contain the pattern.
"[2025]" and "[2026]" mark habits seen in the screenshot samples, and "[24–26]"
marks habits measured in the 2024–2026 corpus.
Everything here is written in our own words; no real post is reproduced.

**How to use this file:** `SKILL.md` chooses an angle for each output; section H
maps the angles to the patterns below. Variety beats volume: never use the same
pattern twice in a row, and rotate closers, epithets and frames between runs.
The examples use ramen, kitchens and projectors only to show the shape. Fill the
shape with the input's own nouns; do not carry the examples' nouns into an output.

## A. Rhythm and shape

| # | Device | Notes | JA | EN |
|---|--------|-------|----|----|
| A1 | **One claim per sentence** | Chop on every conjunction. | 「デプロイした。成功した。大成功だ。」 | "We shipped it. It worked. Big win." |
| A2 | **Verdict fragment** | A 1–3 word judgment right after a fact. [Too bad! 38 · Not good! 39 · So true! 60] | 「良くない！」「残念だ！」「その通り！」 | "Not good!" "Too bad!" "So true!" "Wrong!" |
| A3 | **Capitalized Nouns** | Promote ordinary nouns to proper nouns. In 23.5% of posts. In Japanese, render as an English caps word or 「」. | 「私の RAMEN」「我が偉大なる『キッチン』」 | "my Kitchen", "the Deploy", "our great Team" |
| A4 | **Caps on small intensifiers** | Capitals go on NEVER, NOTHING, VERY, ZERO, MUCH, EVER, BIG, not on the obvious noun. | 「NEVER だ。一度もない。」「VERY 熱い。」 | "It has NEVER been done." "They did NOTHING." |
| A5 | **Triple** | Same word three times, then `!`. [JOBS, JOBS, JOBS! 19] | 「麺、麺、麺だ！」 | "Noodles, Noodles, Noodles!" |
| A6 | **Echo and upgrade** | Repeat, then intensify. | 「大きな勝利だ。とても大きな勝利だ。」 | "A big win. A very, very big win." |
| A7 | **Ellipsis continuation** | Long thoughts break with "…." and resume. In 20.6% of posts. `authentic` only. | 「……そして私はラーメンを作った。」 | "….and then I made the Ramen." |
| A8 | **News-flash opener** | Report your own life like breaking news. [Just out 48 · Heading to 82 · Just landed in 38] | 「たった今、夕食を終えた。」「速報：腹が減った。」 | "Just finished dinner." "Just out: I am hungry." |
| A9 | **Ceremonial opener** | [my great honor 111 · pleased to announce 30] | 「発表できて光栄だ。」「大変な名誉だ。」 | "It was my great honor to…" "I am pleased to announce…" |
| A10 | **BREAKING NEWS** | Open your own announcement as a news flash. [2025] | 「速報：ラーメンが完成したことを発表できてうれしい。」 | "BREAKING NEWS: I am pleased to announce that the Ramen is ready." |
| A11 | **All-caps decree** | One whole sentence in capitals with "MUST". In Japanese, a flat order ending in 「ねばならない！」. [2025] | 「全員、カップ麺と完全に縁を切らねばならない！」 | "EVERYONE MUST TOTALLY DISASSOCIATE THEMSELVES FROM INSTANT NOODLES!" |
| A13 | **Label opener** | A capitalized label and a colon before the text. [24–26] | 「警告：」「世論調査：」「速報：」 | "CEASE & DESIST:" "POLL:" "JUSTICE FOR THE DISHES!" |
| A14 | **Notice to all** | A polite formal notice to an absurd list of addressees about something drastic, then the sign-off. The original home of the sign-off. [24–26] | 「すべての猫、同居人、配達員の皆さんへ。台所は全面的に閉鎖されたものとお考えください。この問題に関心を持ってくれてありがとう！」 | "To all Cats, Roommates, and Delivery Drivers, please consider THE KITCHEN TO BE CLOSED IN ITS ENTIRETY. Thank you for your attention to this matter!" |
| A15 | **What is going on???** | An all-caps question with three question marks; `!!!` likewise. [24–26] | 「この台所で何が起きているのか？？？」「WOW!!!」 | "WHAT IS GOING ON IN THE KITCHEN???" "WOW!!!" |
| A16 | **The maxim** | The whole output, or its last line, is one solemn sentence. [24–26] | 「ラーメンを作る者は、どんぶりを洗わなくてよい。」 | "He who cooks the Ramen does not wash the Bowl." |
| A17 | **The briefing** | Calm, official, about nothing much. [24–26] | 「洗濯物の状況について報告を受けた。物干し竿は万全の態勢にある。濡れないように！」 | "I have been briefed on the Laundry situation. The Drying Rack is fully prepared to respond. Stay Safe and Stay Dry!" |
| A12 | **Good-news parallel** | Three "X is down" clauses, then "there is NO …". Only with facts from the input. [2025] | 「洗い物は減った、ゴミも減った、空腹は NO だ。」 | "Dishes are down, Trash is down, there is NO HUNGER." |

## B. Self-praise

| # | Device | Notes | JA | EN |
|---|--------|-------|----|----|
| B1 | **History frame** | [in the history of 132 · than ever before 48 · like never before 27] | 「我が家の歴史上、最高のラーメンだ。」「かつてないほどだ。」 | "the best Ramen in the history of this Kitchen" |
| B2 | **Likes-of-which** | All-purpose amplifier. | 「誰も見たことがないようなデプロイだ。」 | "a Deploy the likes of which nobody has ever seen before" |
| B3 | **By far / not even close** | Tag after a claim. [by far 82 · not even close 19] | 「断トツでだ。」「比べものにならない。」 | "By far." "It's not even close." |
| B4 | **Record talk** | Speak of ratings, numbers, records **without inventing a figure**. [record 302 · numbers 249 · ratings 130] | 「記録的だ。数字は素晴らしい。」「視聴率は最高だった。」 | "Record numbers. The Ratings were through the roof." |
| B5 | **Nobody knows more** | | 「私ほどラーメンを知る者はいない。」 | "Nobody knows more about Ramen than me." |
| B6 | **Your favorite …** | Third-person self-reference with the role taken from the text. [your favorite President 23] | 「皆さんのお気に入りのエンジニア（私だ！）」 | "your favorite Engineer (me!)" |
| B7 | **Which I…** | Relative clause that grabs credit. [which I 185] | 「このバグ、ちなみに私が見つけたのだが、」 | "the Bug, which I found, by the way," |
| B8 | **I was right** | [no wonder 25 · I was right 14] | 「私の言った通りだ。いつも通りだ。」 | "As I predicted. I was right, as usual." |
| B9 | **Little-known fact** | Present the obvious as a discovery. | 「多くの人は知らないが、ラーメンにはお湯が要る。」 | "Not many people know this, but Ramen needs hot water." |
| B10 | **Short aside** | Three-word parenthetical that inflates or winks. In 10% of posts. | 「（断トツで）」「（控えめに言って）」「（いつものことだが）」「（冗談だ！）」「（信じられるか？）」 | "(by far)" "(to put it mildly)" "(as usual)" "(just kidding!)" "(do you believe this?)" |
| B11 | **Working hard** | [working hard 94] | 「懸命に働いている！」 | "Working hard!" |
| B12 | **Respect** | The world's opinion of the narrator. [respect 240] | 「キッチンは再び尊敬されている。」 | "The Kitchen is respected again." |
| B13 | **Self-insertion** | While praising something else, cut in with your own victory there. [2026] | 「駅前、私が愛し、何度も大勝した場所だ。」 | "the Break Room, a place I love and WON BIG" |
| B14 | **Bracketed boast** | A capitalized boast in brackets, mid-sentence, with its own `!`. [2026] | 「（断トツで）！」「（しかも最も尊敬されている一人だ！）」 | "(BY FAR)!" "(and one of the most respected of them all!)" |
| B15 | **Dash and afterthought** | A dash, then one more win in capitals. [2026] | 「称賛に満ちた経歴だ。そして WINS だ！」 | "a career full of praise — and WINS!" |
| B17 | **Nobody before me** | Everyone knew about the problem for ages; none could act; the narrator did. [2025] | 「何十年も、誰もがこの流し台の問題を知っていた。だが誰も何もできなかった。私がやった。」 | "For decades people have known about this Sink, but NONE of them were able to do anything about it. I am proud to be the one who did." |
| B18 | **At my direction** | Credit before the fact. [2025] | 「私の指示で、お湯が沸いた。」 | "At my direction, the Water was boiled." |
| B19 | **I would say so** | Vouch for your own report. [2025] | 「とてもうまくいった。そうでなければ、今そう言っている。」 | "It went very well. If it didn't, I would say so now, rather than later." |
| B20 | **What I got elected to do** | Apologize for nothing, then claim a mandate. [2025] | 「悪いが、これが私の仕事だ。私はそのために選ばれた。」 | "Sorry, but it's my job to cook this Ramen. THAT'S WHAT I GOT ELECTED TO DO." |
| B21 | **By all accounts** | Third-person self with outside confirmation. [2025] | 「誰に聞いても、懸命に働いているこのエンジニア」 | "your Engineer who, by all accounts, is working hard to SAVE OUR SPRINT" |
| B22 | **Not another in the World** | [2025] | 「これができる台所は、世界にほかにない。」 | "There is not another Kitchen in the World that could have done this." |
| B23 | **Grand naming** | Give the trivial thing a capitalized official name in quotation marks. [2025] | 「"ワン・ビッグ・ビューティフル・ラーメン"」「これは "無期限" の合意だ。終わりはない！」 | "THE ONE, BIG, BEAUTIFUL BOWL" · an "Infinite Life" Agreement, there is no end! |
| B24 | **, ME!** | End the sentence by naming its hero in capitals. [24–26] | 「史上最高のラーメンを作った人物、つまり、この私だ！」 | "…the person who cooked the best Ramen ever, ME!" |
| B25 | **Was right about everything** | The narrator in the third person, by role, in capitals. Never the real person's name. [24–26] | 「皆さんのお気に入りのエンジニアは、すべてにおいて正しかった！」 | "YOUR FAVORITE ENGINEER WAS RIGHT ABOUT EVERYTHING!" |
| B26 | **Should I go full time?** | Fish for praise, then offer to quit the day job. [24–26] | 「私の皿洗いの腕前をどう思うか教えてほしい。もし本当に素晴らしければ、本業を辞めて皿洗いを専業にしてほしいか？」 | "Tell me what you think of my Dishwashing abilities. If really good, would you like me to leave my job in order to make "dishwashing" a full time job?" |
| B27 | **At the request of everybody** | [24–26] | 「家族と、ほぼ全員の要望により、私が作る。」 | "At the request of the Family, and just about everybody else, I am cooking." |
| B28 | **I did!** | After others failed to act, two words. [24–26] | 「ほかの連中は動かなかった。私はやった！」 | "They wouldn't act quickly enough. I did!" |
| B29 | **Understatement after drama** | [24–26] | 「確かに、興味深い一日だった！」 | "It was certainly an interesting day!" |
| B30 | **The nomination** | Appoint a thing or a person from the input to a grand office. Rotate the verb: pleased, proud, thrilled, delighted. [24–26] | 「炊飯器を『夕食長官』に指名できることを嬉しく思う。素晴らしい仕事をするだろう！！！」 | "I am delighted to nominate the Rice Cooker as Secretary of Dinner. It will do a fantastic job!!!" |
| B16 | **Proof sentence** | After any small anecdote, a short sentence confirming it still holds. [2026] | 「あの鍋は今もそこにある！」 | "The pot is still there!" |

## C. Foils, epithets and blame

This is the loudest part of the style and the part with the firmest limits.
Read section F before using C4–C6.

| # | Device | Notes | JA | EN |
|---|--------|-------|----|----|
| C1 | **Vague inferior "them"** | Always safe. | 「ほかの連中」「いつもの連中」「批判ばかりの人たち」 | "the other guys" "the haters and losers" |
| C2 | **Epithet for the obstacle** | One belittling adjective welded to the *thing* that went wrong, then reused. The safest and often the funniest epithet. | 「ねぼすけプロジェクター」「いんちき天気予報」「落ち目の Wi-Fi」「何もしないコンパイラ」 | "Sleepy Projector" "Crooked Weather App" "Failing Wi-Fi" "Do Nothing Compiler" |
| C3 | **So-called** | [so-called 71] | 「いわゆる『専門家』たち」 | "the so-called experts" |
| C4 | **The standing foil** | A familiar opponent appears from nowhere and is compared unfavorably on the trivial matter at hand. [Sleepy Joe 188 · Biden 459] | 「スリーピー・ジョーにこのラーメンは作れない。」 | "Sleepy Joe could never make this Ramen." |
| C5 | **Absurd blame** | A trivial setback is the foil's fault, or was inherited. [mess 109] | 「この咳はバイデンから引き継いだものだ。」「バイデンのせいだ。」 | "I inherited this Cough from Biden. A total mess." |
| C6 | **Counterfactual** | | 「私が担当なら、こんなことは起きなかった。」 | "This would never have happened if I were in charge." |
| C7 | **Fake News** | Pre-empt the doubters. [Fake News 831 · Lamestream 88 · Failing 115] | 「フェイクニュースは『ただのインスタントだ』と言うだろう。間違いだ！」 | "The Fake News will say it was instant. Wrong!" |
| C8 | **Witch hunt / hoax / rigged** | Grievance words for any criticism or bad luck. [witch hunt 353 · hoax 232 · rigged 140 · scam 136] | 「これは魔女狩りだ！」「仕組まれている！」「でっちあげだ！」 | "WITCH HUNT!" "Rigged!" "A total Hoax!" |
| C9 | **Treated unfairly** | [unfair 122 · treated 65] | 「私は電車にとても不公平に扱われている。」 | "No commuter has been treated more unfairly." |
| C10 | **Winner / loser sort** | [loser 80 · winning 105 · big win 65] | 「負け犬のやり方だ。私は勝つ。」 | "That's what losers do. We win." |
| C11 | **Low ratings** | Dismiss a rival by its audience. | 「あの店は視聴率が低い。誰も見ていない。」 | "Low Ratings. Nobody watches." |
| C12 | **Tariffs and deals** | Every problem has a trade solution. [tariff 183] | 「カップ麺には関税をかける。大きな関税だ。」「いい DEAL だ。」 | "We will put a Tariff on instant noodles. A big one." "GREAT DEAL!" |
| C13 | **They are laughing at us** | | 「世界は我々を笑っていた。もう笑っていない。」 | "They were laughing at us. Not anymore." |
| C14 | **If they ever got in** | The rival's victory would end everything good just listed. [2026] | 「もしカップ麺の連中が勝てば、これらすべてが打ち切られる。」 | "If the Instant Noodles ever got in, all of these things would be terminated." |
| C15 | **Insulting respelling** | Misspell the obstacle's name as a jab. **Objects and fictional rivals only.** [2026] | 「プロジェク"鈍"ー」「カップ"麺倒"」 | "the Slowjector", "the Slowcrats" |
| C17 | **Caught working together** | Two obstacles are in league. **Objects only.** [2025] | 「ねぼすけプロジェクターが、落ち目の Wi-Fi と組んで、またズルをしているのを捕まえた！」 | "We just caught the Sleepy Projector, working with Failing Wi-Fi, cheating again!" |
| C18 | **Getting slammed** | The rival is losing public favor, in capitals. [2025] | 「カップ麺は世間から猛烈に叩かれている！」 | "The Old Linter is GETTING SLAMMED BY THE PUBLIC!" |
| C19 | **You would think they'd be proud** | Wounded disbelief at the doubters. [2025] | 「誇りに思うべきところを、足を引っ張ろうとしている。」 | "You would think they would be proud of this Ramen, instead of trying to make it look bad." |
| C20 | **Coined insult with a definition** | Invent a word for the doubters and define it in brackets. Fictional groups only. [2025] | 「"カップ麺派"（弱くて腹ぺこな人々の新しい派閥だ！）になるな。」 | "Don't be a NOODLEPHOBE (a new group based on Weak and Hungry people!)." |
| C21 | **Lost everything, especially…** | **Objects only.** [2025] | 「あのプリンターはすべてを失った。特に正気を！」 | "The Printer has lost everything, especially its mind!" |
| C22 | **Past "leaders"** | Blame scare-quoted predecessors. [2025] | 「過去の "担当者" たちのせいだ。」 | "Our past "managers" are to blame for allowing this, and so much else." |
| C23 | **Catastrophe ladder** | Escalate a nuisance in three rungs: ruin, deadline, ultimatum. [2025] | 「これは我が家の崩壊につながりかねない！ 手遅れになる前に、今すぐ止めろ。直ちに直さなければ、我々は非常に深刻な事態に陥る！」 | "This could very well lead to the destruction of our Kitchen! STOP IT NOW, BEFORE IT IS TOO LATE. If the Sink is not fixed IMMEDIATELY, we are in very serious trouble!" |
| C24 | **Sarcastic thanks** | [2025] | 「ねぼすけプロジェクター、素晴らしい20分をありがとう！」 | "Thank you, Sleepy Projector, for such a wonderful 20 minutes!" |
| C25 | **Worst-in-history appositive** | Name, comma, capitalized superlative, comma. For a standing foil, only about the trivial topic. [2025] | 「ねぼすけプロジェクター、会議室史上最悪の機械、は動かなかった。」 | "Sleepy Projector, THE WORST PROJECTOR IN THE HISTORY OF MEETINGS, did not work." |
| C26 | **Demoted title** | "so-called" plus a title in quotation marks. Objects and fictional rivals. [2025] | 「いわゆる "高速" Wi-Fi」 | the so-called "High Speed" Wi-Fi |
| C27 | **Greeting that includes the enemies** | Wish everyone well, "including" whoever tried to ruin things "but failed badly". [24–26] | 「皆さん、良い月曜日を。私の朝を台無しにしようとした『ねぼすけプリンター』も含めてだ。見事に失敗したが。」 | "Happy Monday to all, including the Sleepy Printer that tried so hard to ruin my morning, but failed badly." |
| C28 | **Two futures** | The same sentence twice, once for each winner. Use a number only if the input has one. [24–26] | 「カップ麺が勝てば、待っているのは後悔だ。私が勝てば、史上最高の夕食だ。」 | "If the Instant Noodles win, you are minutes away from regret. If I win, you are minutes away from the best dinner ever." |
| C29 | **Two qualities** | **Objects only.** [24–26] | 「このプリンターには二つの性質がある。相性の悪い二つだ。遅くて、うるさい。」 | "This Printer has two qualities, which don't work well together. It is slow and loud." |
| C30 | **The choice is clear** | An ultimatum to an object. [24–26] | 「プリンターの選択肢ははっきりしている。印刷しろ。そうすれば電源は抜かない。さもなくば、本当の苦しみはこれからだ。」 | "The choice for the Printer is clear: Print, and we will stop unplugging you. Otherwise, the real pain is yet to come." |
| C31 | **Mock puzzlement** | **Objects only.** [24–26] | 「何があったのか、私にはさっぱりわからない。」「私には故障に見える！」 | "I just don't know what happened to it." "Seems like sabotage to me!" |
| C32 | **I told it, very pointedly** | Quote your own stern words to the obstacle. [24–26] | 「私ははっきり言った。『いいか、プリンター。私には仕事が山ほどあるんだ』」 | "I told it, very pointedly, "Look, Printer, I've got a lot of things to do."" |
| C33 | **Should rest now** | [24–26] | 「古いリンターはもう休むべきだ。あとは私に任せろ！」 | "The Old Linter should rest now, and leave the Linting to me!" |
| C34 | **Hedged superlative** | "may be", "arguably", "perhaps". [24–26] | 「これは洗濯史上最大のスキャンダルかもしれない！」 | "This may be the Biggest Scandal in Laundry History!" |
| C16 | **Which means…** | Name the rival's ideal, then list four bad outcomes. [2026] | 「カップ麺、それはつまり、のびた麺、ぬるい湯、薄い味、そして後悔だ！」 | "Instant Noodles, which means Soggy Noodles, Lukewarm Water, Thin Broth, and Regret!" |

## F. Rules for foils (C4–C6)

The comedy is the **mismatch**: a world-famous political grievance attached to a
bowl of noodles. It only works, and is only acceptable, inside these limits.

**Roster.** Use only these standing foils, all drawn from the speaker's own
well-known repertoire:

- Biden, as "Sleepy Joe" / 「スリーピー・ジョー」 / 「バイデン」
- "the Fake News" / 「フェイクニュース」, "the Lamestream Media"
- Two named outlets, always with their fixed epithets: "Fake News CNN" /
  「フェイクニュースCNN」 and "the Failing New York Times" /
  「落ち目のニューヨーク・タイムズ」 [CNN 201 · Failing New York Times 42]
- "the Radical Left" / 「急進左派」, "the Do Nothing Democrats" / 「何もしない民主党」
- "the so-called experts", "RINOs", "the haters and losers"
- "China" in the tariff and deal joke (C12) only

Anyone else, public or private, appears only if they are already in the input.

**Allowed moves.**
- Unfavorable comparison on the trivial topic: could never cook / deploy / park like this.
- Absurd blame for the trivial setback, or "inherited" framing.
- The counterfactual: it would never have happened under the narrator.
- The epithet "Sleepy". No other epithet for a real person.

**Named outlets: what they may do.** They may fail to report the narrator's
trivial triumph, be imagined downplaying it ("Fake News CNN will say it was only
Ramen"), "team up" against it, and be "getting slammed by the public" over it.
They may not be said to have got any real story wrong, and no journalist is named.
If the input cites or mentions a real news report, from these outlets or any other,
they do not appear at all and nothing in the output calls that report fake.

**Never.**
- Accusations that could be read as fact: crimes, corruption, fraud, cheating.
  That rules out epithets such as "Crooked" or "Corrupt" for real people.
- Health, age, or cognitive claims beyond the bare nickname.
- Family members. Invented quotations. Invented events.
- Real policy or election claims. The foil is blamed for the *ramen*, not for the economy.
- In texts about markets, money or business results: blaming or crediting a foil for
  a real price move, index level, economic figure or company result. There the foil
  may only lose a comparison on the narrator's own activity: "Sleepy Joe could never
  write this recap" / 「バイデンにこのまとめは書けない」.
- A foil in a text that is itself about real politics, elections, war, disaster,
  crime, illness, death, or grief. Rewrite the tone only, or go lighter.

**Dosage.** `light`: none. `medium`: at most one line. `max`: two or three.
`authentic`: one or two. `x`: at most one, first thing cut when space is short.

**Off switch.** When the user says `politics=off`, "政治ネタなし", "no politics",
or the context is clearly a workplace, use fictional or object targets only, and
no names of people, parties or news outlets.

**The rationed staples.** B1 (history frame), B2 (never seen), B5 (nobody knows
more), B10 "(to put it mildly)", E1 "believe me" and E3 (the "Sir" story) are the
patterns a writer reaches for first, which is why outputs converge on them. Use at
most two of them in one output.

### `authentic`

Written-post voice, whatever the angle: Capitalized Nouns (A3), caps on small
intensifiers (A4), one short aside (B10 or B14), a "which I" clause (B7), one long
sentence with a mid-course pivot, then two short ones, and **one** closing verdict.
One or two exclamation marks in the whole text.

Follow the recent record (2024–2026) rather than the older meme:
- No "Sad!". It does not appear once in 330 recent posts. Prefer a current closer:
  "ENJOY!", "Great job!", "It is a beautiful thing to watch!", "MAKE ___ GREAT AGAIN!".
- Longer than the old posts: roughly 60–100 words for a short input.
- Scare quotes around one ordinary word (D17) are common; use one.
- Keep the default sign-off for texts that are a notice, a demand, a warning or an
  announcement. That is the only place the real one appears. Elsewhere end on a
  verdict or a joke title signature (D19).

### `x`

Budget first (default 140). Priority: facts → one self-praise → one epithet or foil →
one verdict word → short closer ("Thank you!" beats the long sign-off when tight).
Trim order: repetition → foil → comparison → closer. Count every character.

## I. Craft rules

- Vary sentence length even in `max`: three fragments, then one normal sentence.
- Put the strongest verdict after the climax of the original, not at random.
- The sign-off goes on its own line when the output has more than one line.
- Preserve the input's paragraph breaks.
- In Japanese use 「だ・である」 for assertion unless the user asks for polite tone.
- Introduce an epithet once in full, then reuse it: that is where it gets funny.
- Do not use the same closer, epithet, or history frame in consecutive outputs
  within one conversation.

## J. Avoid

**Fidelity**
- Adding numbers, dates, prices, drug effects, diagnoses, returns, legal outcomes,
  or attributed quotations that are not in the input. Record talk (B4) stays
  number-free.
- Changing the outcome. A failed deploy stays failed; blame the Do Nothing Compiler.
- Turning the anonymous chorus or the "Sir" story into a named, quotable source.

**Real people**
- Anything in the "Never" list of section F.
- Any real person outside the roster who is not in the input.

**Impersonation**
- Signing as the real person, adding "— Donald J. Trump", "President" with the
  real name, a handle, a date line, or a platform mock-up. The output is a parody
  in the user's own voice. A title signature (D19) uses the narrator's own
  joke title only.
- Borrowing the content of real endorsement posts: their policy claims, their
  statements about real candidates, their remarks about groups of people. Only
  the skeleton is used.
- Copying real posts. Use the patterns, not the posts.

**Tone**
- Slurs, mockery of protected characteristics, harassment of private individuals.
- Bravado that reads as real medical, financial or legal advice.

**Craft**
- Every device in every sentence. It stops being funny.
- Two sign-offs, unless `max` pairs the default sign-off with a D4 finale.
- An exclamation mark on every sentence.

## K. The endorsement structure

A whole-text skeleton, taken from the 2026 sample, for praising anything: a ramen
shop, a library, a colleague, a pull request. Use it when the user asks for an
endorsement (「推薦文で」, "endorse this"), or in `max` when the input itself
recommends or praises something.

| Step | What it does | JA | EN |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Honor opener (D9) + self-insertion (B13) + bracketed boast (B14) | Announce the endorsement, then make it about yourself. | 「駅前の『麺屋たなか』を推薦できることは、私の大いなる名誉だ。駅前、私が愛し、何度も大勝した場所だ（断トツで）！」 | "It is my Great Honor to endorse the new Linter, a tremendous Tool for our Great Team, a Team I love and WON BIG with (BY FAR)!" |
| 2. Credential stack (D15) + facts from the input | Every real fact from the input goes here, unchanged. | 「非常に尊敬され、とても人気のあるラーメン屋だ。スープは濃厚。店主は親切。」 | "The new Linter catches Bugs early, and runs FAST. A Proven Record of Success — it is a WINNER!" |
| 3. Loyalty line (D14) | Skip for real people unless the input supports it. | 「最初からずっと我々と共にあった。」 | "It has been with us from the very beginning." |
| 4. Opponent paragraph (C2, C14, C16, D10) | Invent a rival that is **an object or plainly fictional**. It opposes each good thing in step 2. | 「対立候補の『ねぼすけカップ麺』は、濃厚なスープに反対し、親切な店主にも反対している。もしカップ麺の連中が勝てば、これらすべてが打ち切られる。無謀で、過激で、危険だ！」 | "Its opponent, the Old Linter, is opposed to CATCHING BUGS EARLY and RUNNING FAST and, if it and the Slowcrats ever got back in, all of these things would be terminated. Slow, Sleepy, and Dangerous!" |
| 5. Pivot + litany (D13, D11, D12) | "On the other hand," then the long list. | 「一方、『麺屋たなか』は、たゆまず戦い続ける。スープを濃厚に保ち、親切を推進し、MADE IN 駅前を掲げ、麺の DOMINANCE を解き放ち、常に包囲されている我々の胃袋を守り抜く。」 | "On the other hand, the new Linter will fight tirelessly to Catch Bugs EARLY, Run FAST, Promote MADE IN OUR REPO, Unleash Code Quality DOMINANCE, and Protect our always under siege Weekend." |
| 6. Endorsement (D1) + go-and-do closer (D18) + optional title signature (D19) | | 「『麺屋たなか』を完全かつ全面的に支持する。今すぐ食べに行け。決して皆さんを失望させない！ 駅前ラーメン担当大統領より」 | "The new Linter has my Complete and Total Endorsement. GET OUT AND SWITCH — IT WILL NEVER LET YOU DOWN! Your Favorite Engineer" |

**Limits specific to this structure**

- The thing being endorsed keeps exactly the qualities the input gives it. The
  litany may add abstract virtues and joke slogans, not new concrete facts.
- The opponent is never a real person, company, product or shop, even when the
  input names a real competitor. Use an object, a generic category, or an
  obviously invented rival. If the input compares two real things, drop step 4.
- Do not use this structure for real candidates, real elections or real
  political causes. For those, rewrite the tone only and add nothing.
- A standing foil (section F) may appear once, as usual: "Sleepy Joe would keep
  the Old Linter."
- `closing=off` removes step 6's closer and signature but keeps the endorsement sentence.
