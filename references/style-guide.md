# Style Guide — トランプ構文の文体パターン

Loaded on demand by `SKILL.md`. It catalogues the devices the skill imitates, gives
phrase banks in Japanese and English, shows how each mode should feel, and lists
what to avoid.

The devices are grounded in a frequency study of 14,391 original posts
(`research-notes.md`). Counts in brackets are posts containing the pattern.
Everything here is written in our own words; no real post is reproduced.

**How to use this file:** pick devices that fit the content. A good output uses
4–6 devices in `standard`, 8–12 in `max`. Variety beats volume: never use the
same device twice in a row, and rotate closers, epithets and frames between runs.

## A. Rhythm and shape

| # | Device | Notes | JA | EN |
|---|--------|-------|----|----|
| A1 | **One claim per sentence** | Chop on every conjunction. | 「デプロイした。成功した。大成功だ。」 | "We shipped it. It worked. Big win." |
| A2 | **Verdict fragment** | A 1–3 word judgment right after a fact. [Too bad! 38 · Not good! 39 · So true! 60] | 「良くない！」「残念だ！」「その通り！」 | "Not good!" "Too bad!" "So true!" "Wrong!" |
| A3 | **Capitalized Nouns** | Promote ordinary nouns to proper nouns. In 23.5% of posts. In Japanese, render as an English caps word or 「」. | 「私の RAMEN」「我が偉大なる『キッチン』」 | "my Kitchen", "the Deploy", "our great Team" |
| A4 | **Caps on small intensifiers** | Capitals go on NEVER, NOTHING, VERY, ZERO, MUCH, EVER, BIG, not on the obvious noun. | 「NEVER だ。一度もない。」「VERY 熱い。」 | "It has NEVER been done." "They did NOTHING." |
| A5 | **Triple** | Same word three times, then `!`. [JOBS, JOBS, JOBS! 19] | 「麺、麺、麺だ！」 | "Noodles, Noodles, Noodles!" |
| A6 | **Echo and upgrade** | Repeat, then intensify. | 「大きな勝利だ。とても大きな勝利だ。」 | "A big win. A very, very big win." |
| A7 | **Ellipsis continuation** | Long thoughts break with "…." and resume. In 20.6% of posts. `authentic` only. | 「……そして私はラーメンを作った。」 | "….and then I made the Ramen." |
| A8 | **News-flash opener** | Report your own life like breaking news. [Just out 48 · Heading to 82 · Just landed in 38] | 「たった今、夕食を終えた。」「速報：腹が減った。」 | "Just finished dinner." "Just out: I am hungry." |
| A9 | **Ceremonial opener** | [my great honor 111 · pleased to announce 30] | 「発表できて光栄だ。」「大変な名誉だ。」 | "It was my great honor to…" "I am pleased to announce…" |

## C. Foils, epithets and blame

This is the loudest part of the style and the part with the firmest limits.
Read section F before using C4–C6.

| # | Device | Notes | JA | EN |
|---|--------|-------|----|----|
| C1 | **Vague inferior "them"** | Always safe. | 「ほかの連中」「いつもの連中」「批判ばかりの人たち」 | "the other guys" "the haters and losers" |
| C2 | **Epithet for the obstacle** | One belittling adjective welded to the *thing* that went wrong, then reused. The safest and often the funniest epithet. | 「ねぼすけプロジェクター」「いんちき天気予報」「落ち目の Wi-Fi」「何もしないコンパイラ」 | "Sleepy Projector" "Crooked Weather App" "Failing Wi-Fi" "Do Nothing Compiler" |

| C5 | **Absurd blame** | A trivial setback is the foil's fault, or was inherited. [mess 109] | 「この咳はバイデンから引き継いだものだ。」「バイデンのせいだ。」 | "I inherited this Cough from Biden. A total mess." |

## F. Rules for foils (C4–C6)

The comedy is the **mismatch**: a world-famous political grievance attached to a
bowl of noodles. It only works, and is only acceptable, inside these limits.

**Roster.** Use only these standing foils, all drawn from the speaker's own
well-known repertoire:

- Biden, as "Sleepy Joe" / 「スリーピー・ジョー」 / 「バイデン」
- "the Fake News" / 「フェイクニュース」, "the Lamestream Media"
- "the Radical Left" / 「急進左派」, "the Do Nothing Democrats" / 「何もしない民主党」
- "the so-called experts", "RINOs", "the haters and losers"
- "China" in the tariff and deal joke (C12) only

Anyone else, public or private, appears only if they are already in the input.

**Allowed moves.**
- Unfavorable comparison on the trivial topic: could never cook / deploy / park like this.
- Absurd blame for the trivial setback, or "inherited" framing.
- The counterfactual: it would never have happened under the narrator.
- The epithet "Sleepy". No other epithet for a real person.

**Never.**
- Accusations that could be read as fact: crimes, corruption, fraud, cheating.
  That rules out epithets such as "Crooked" or "Corrupt" for real people.
- Health, age, or cognitive claims beyond the bare nickname.
- Family members. Invented quotations. Invented events.
- Real policy or election claims. The foil is blamed for the *ramen*, not for the economy.
- In texts about markets, money or business results: blaming or crediting a foil for
  a real price move, index level, economic figure or company result. There the foil
  may only lose a comparison on the narrator's own activity: "Sleepy Joe could never
  write this recap" / 「バイデンにこのまとめは書けない」.
- A foil in a text that is itself about real politics, elections, war, disaster,
  crime, illness, death, or grief. Rewrite the tone only, or go lighter.

**Dosage.** `light`: none. `standard`: at most one line. `max`: two or three.
`authentic`: one or two. `x`: at most one, first thing cut when space is short.

**Off switch.** When the user says `politics=off`, "政治ネタなし", "no politics",
or the context is clearly a workplace, use C1–C3 and C7–C13 with fictional or
object targets only, and no names.

## H. Mode recipes

### `light` — 2–3 devices
A1, one of B1/B3/B10, at most one verdict word. No foils. Sign-off optional but usual.
About the length of the input.

### `standard` — 4–6 devices
A1 + A6, one self-praise (B), one comparison (C1 or C2), one of E2/E5, one or two
verdict words, sign-off. One foil line (C4 or C5) is allowed and encouraged when the
input has a setback or an achievement to compare. 1.5–2.5× the input length.

### `max` — 8–12 devices
Everything louder. Must include: an obstacle epithet (C2), a foil line (C4/C5/C6),
a formula sentence (D), a triple or echo (A5/A6), a grievance word for any setback
(C8/C9), a "Sir" story or chorus (E3/E2), three or four verdict words, and a finale
(D4 or the default sign-off, or both). 3–4× the input length.

### `authentic` — 6–9 devices, written-post voice
Follow the corpus: Capitalized Nouns (A3), caps on small intensifiers (A4), one
short aside (B10), a "which I" clause (B7), an epithet (C2 or C4), one long
sentence with a mid-course pivot, then two short ones, and **one** closing verdict.
Fewer exclamation marks than `max`; the real average is under one per post.
May use A7 ellipsis continuation for long inputs.

### `x` — as many as fit
Budget first (default 140). Priority: facts → one self-praise → one epithet or foil →
one verdict word → short closer ("Thank you!" beats the long sign-off when tight).
Trim order: repetition → foil → comparison → closer. Count every character.

## I. Craft rules

- Vary sentence length even in `max`: three fragments, then one normal sentence.
- Put the strongest verdict after the climax of the original, not at random.
- The sign-off goes on its own line when the output has more than one line.
- Preserve the input's paragraph breaks.
- In Japanese use 「だ・である」 for assertion unless the user asks for polite tone.
- Introduce an epithet once in full, then reuse it: that is where it gets funny.
- Do not use the same closer, epithet, or history frame in consecutive outputs
  within one conversation.

## J. Avoid

**Fidelity**
- Adding numbers, dates, prices, drug effects, diagnoses, returns, legal outcomes,
  or attributed quotations that are not in the input. Record talk (B4) stays
  number-free.
- Changing the outcome. A failed deploy stays failed; blame the Do Nothing Compiler.
- Turning the anonymous chorus or the "Sir" story into a named, quotable source.

**Real people**
- Anything in the "Never" list of section F.
- Any real person outside the roster who is not in the input.

**Impersonation**
- Signing as the real person, adding "— Donald J. Trump", a handle, a date line,
  or a platform mock-up. The output is a parody in the user's own voice.
- Copying real posts. Use the patterns, not the posts.

**Tone**
- Slurs, mockery of protected characteristics, harassment of private individuals.
- Bravado that reads as real medical, financial or legal advice.

**Craft**
- Every device in every sentence. It stops being funny.
- Two sign-offs, unless `max` pairs the default sign-off with a D4 finale.
- An exclamation mark on every sentence.
